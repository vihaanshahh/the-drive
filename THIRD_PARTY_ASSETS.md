# Third-party assets

The humanoid character mesh and skin/eye textures are adapted from **Superhero Male** in **Universal Base Characters — Standard** by **Quaternius**.

- Official source: https://quaternius.com/packs/universalbasecharacters.html
- Official download: https://quaternius.itch.io/universal-base-characters
- License: **CC0 1.0 Universal**, https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
- Original license is retained in `TheDrive/Assets/QUATERNIUS_LICENSE.txt`.

The selected glTF geometry was converted to the compact `DriverMesh.json` manifest and `DriverMesh.bin` vertex/index/skin-weight buffers. The original smooth mesh, UVs, normals, humanoid hierarchy, joint weights and inverse-bind matrices are preserved. Unused UV sets, vertex colors, source animation metadata and unselected models were omitted. Skin and eye textures were resized to 512 and 128 pixels. Clothing regions are assigned game materials; reversible driving-to-gunner animation is authored in Swift using the original skeleton. These are game-ready stylized characters with human proportions, not photorealistic scans.

The terrain, particles, environment lighting, and player Jeep are generated by this project’s code. Imported pursuing vehicles and town architecture are credited below.

## Imported vehicles

The rally hatchback and pursuing SUV use the **hatchback-sports** and **suv** meshes from **Car Kit** by **Kenney**, under **CC0 1.0 Universal**.

- Official source and license: https://kenney.nl/assets/car-kit
- Creator's download mirror used: https://kenney-assets.itch.io/car-kit
- Original license retained in `TheDrive/Assets/KENNEY_LICENSE.txt`.
- Native resources: `Vehicle-hatchback-sports.json/.bin`, `Vehicle-suv.json/.bin`, and shared `VehicleColor.png`.

Conversion triangulates the original OBJ faces and preserves their sculpted bodywork, normals, palette UVs, door/window outlines, and individual wheel groups. Models are scaled to the game's road, rotated to face forward, and wheels are recentered around their actual axles for steering and rotation. The runtime shares immutable geometry/materials; imported meshes remain the visible vehicle body. Working headlights and the existing animated occupants are added in Swift. Kenney vehicles are stylized game assets, not photorealistic scans. The player Jeep remains original procedural art with an open cabin and mounted gun.

Vehicle palette colors are baked into the `Vehicle-*-colors.bin` RGBA vertex buffers to avoid atlas-orientation differences between importers. RGB values are converted from the source palette’s sRGB space to linear light for SceneKit vertex-color shading; alpha remains linear and unchanged. Kenney's original glazing, grille, tire and trim palette regions are preserved; the body-paint palette column is recolored burnt orange for the rally hatchback and desert sand for the SUV. The source `VehicleColor.png` palette is retained for provenance.

## Imported town architecture

Six house meshes plus compound-fence meshes come from **City Kit (Suburban)** by **Kenney**, licensed **CC0 1.0 Universal**.

- Official source: https://kenney.nl/assets/city-kit-suburban
- Creator's download mirror used: https://kenney-assets.itch.io/city-kit-suburban
- Original license: `TheDrive/Assets/KENNEY_BUILDINGS_LICENSE.txt`.
- Native resources: `EnvironmentMesh.json` and `EnvironmentMesh.bin` (about 930 KB combined).

Selected source meshes are `house_type01`, `house_type02`, `house_type06`, `house_type08`, `house_type10`, `house_type17`, `fence_large`, and `fence_open`. Conversion preserves their authored roofs, porch columns, door/window recesses, chimneys and dormers; centered, scaled triangle buffers are shared by every instance. Original material regions are recolored with sandstone, clay, timber and weathered trim in linear vertex-color space. Roof-damaged variants omit selected original roof triangles; the original geometry remains recognizable. Actual source window faces are kept separately for warm night emission. No replacement box-house geometry is used. Seeded districts vary intact village streets, ruined compounds, sparse outskirts and open desert.

The asphalt, shoulder terrain, paint ribbons and tire-contact simulation use the same analytic `RoadSurface` height field. Roads are generated as subdivided meshes rather than flat boxes. Building foundations sample both footprint ends so their bases meet the sloping terrain.
